# 3D renderer: integrated art review (2026-10-17)

A review of the 3D renderer as merged (master `1cb04eb`), from 23 screenshots of the real game (a 200-turn AI game with the corrupted Svartalfar heartland, a crowded turn-380 game, real fog of war) and the lab scenes, at 40, 56, 96 and 168 px a plot. Three critics (art direction, gameplay readability, technical artifacts) reported 21 findings; triage merged them to 20; a skeptical verifier per finding, told to refute unless the evidence held, confirmed 16 and refuted 4. Screenshot paths (`review/...`) refer to the review's scratch renders, which are not kept: re-render with `tools/r3d-compare.mjs` and `tools/r3d-lab.mjs` (docs/R3D.md §10). **All 16 confirmed findings are now fixed (2026-10-17); each entry below carries a "Fixed" note with what changed and how it was verified.** A fresh adversarial review (2026-10-18, critics then a skeptical verifier per finding) re-checked every fix and found three residual low/medium items — the F6 fix dropped the stack count at the two closest zooms, the F15 fix let the grid overlay bleed one band past the north edge, and the per-pass benchmark did not disable the new quality ladder while measuring — all three now fixed (see the "Follow-up" notes on F6 and F15 and §11 of docs/R3D.md).

Owner decisions that bound the fixes: the map is always in daylight (afternoon or day; no dusk or night); 3D stays opt-in until validated on real GPUs.

## Critics' verdicts

- **art**: As an art director I see real strengths: the Bannor walled town, the coasts and the snowy mountains. But at the 96 px gameplay zoom the frame reads as an outlined, dark-blotched 3D render, not an illustration. Long near-black afternoon shadows stain the land and sea. The vertical slice's own subjects, the Svartalfar cities and the corrupted heartland, are its weakest reads, and the 2D pixel art shows both more clearly. Settlements lose their hierarchy, forests read as clusters of separately inked clip-art trees, and remembered fogged land turns to grey sludge. Every point below survived a check against the obvious reply; four extra renders support them (in review/extra/).
- **readability**: At the gameplay zoom of 96 px a plot, the 3D map gives the broad picture but loses most of the per-plot and per-unit detail a strategy player scans for. The fog of war, the selection frame, city plates, rivers, roads, peaks and the different kinds of forest all read well. But hills, flood plains and resources nearly disappear, unit types are mostly unreadable, foreign stacks show the wrong unit in front, and small cities blend into town improvements behind their garrisons. The 2D art renderer is clearly more legible on the same scenes. The two worst problems are the wrong front unit on foreign stacks and the near-invisible hills. Extra renders are in scratchpad/review/extra/; terrain96.png …
- **technical**: Through the technical-artifact lens, the renderer is mostly clean on land. Props sit on the ground and I found no z-fighting, shadow acne or haloes worth reporting. The real defects are at the water, the map edge and in thin or coarse geometry. The most serious one: every ground mark on a sea plot (selection, hover and target frames, the ships' team rings, path dashes, the grid and borders) is drawn on the sea floor, below the water. So the marks show faded and shifted away from both the ships and the point picking returns. The north edge of every real map also leaves a 12-row black gap before the parchment, and polar ice is painted as a sand beach. Beyond those: there is no anti-aliasing …

- **Evidence:** slice/3d-painterly.png: the lower-left quadrant, west of the river, has navy-black smears across the grassland and plains (cast by trees and hills).
