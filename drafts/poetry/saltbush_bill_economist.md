# Saltbush Bill, Economist

*(Being a digression, in the manner of the Overland, upon the profits of droving)*

'Twas Saltbush Bill, of the Overland, was camped on the Barwon side,
With a mob of travelling wethers spread a half of a county wide,
And a new-chum lad from a city bank, sent bush to learn the trade,
Says, "Bill, I've added the wages up — and where is the money made?"
Says Saltbush Bill, "It's a twisting tale, like the track of a travelling ewe;
It wanders worse than the routes I ride — but I'll try and see it through.

"Now the law of the land, as the squatters wrote, is simple and plain to say:
You shift your sheep on the half-mile road a six-mile stage a day.
But nobody says which miles they'll be, so the six are always found
Where the feed is bare as a shearing floor — and we camp on the good green ground;
And every blade that a wether crops along of the route is free,
Which is why the squatter can't be civil to travelling sheep — or me.
(I had a mate at Come-by-Chance could talk on the subject still,
But that's a yarn of a night in itself, and a bottle to pay the bill.)

"The wages, lad? There's thirty bob a week for the drover's hand,
And the cook draws more — for a cranky cook can poison the Overland;
The boss is paid by the travelling head, so much for a hundred mile,
And he stands the rations and stands the plant, and the agent takes his pile;
For every head that the tally drops when they count at the buyer's gate
Comes out of the boss's cheque alone — so the wise ones watch and wait.

"But here's the arithmetic of the track, that they don't teach in town:
A mob rushed through is skin and bone, and the buyers do 'em down;
A mob brought slow is rolling fat, and it's fat that fetches the price,
And the fat is grass — the squatter's grass! — so dawdle's my advice.
(There's a science, son, in going slow, there's a genius in delay;
I made one trip in a record time — near a year, from Longreach to Hay.)

"There's times an overseer rides down to dust you along the track,
And it pays — it pays like a Sydney bank — to stand and argue back;
I fought a man on the Hard Times Run from breakfast to setting sun,
And I let him win when the light gave out, when the feeding-off was done;
He rode for home with a champion's air, a mighty man of pride,
While behind him, quiet, the travelling sheep spread another half-mile wide.

"And where does it go, the drover's cheque, at the end of the thousand mile?
Well, the publican out at the railhead pub, he greets me with a smile;
The cheque that followed a thousand sheep from the Cooper down to the sea
Cuts out in a week, with the bar shouted twice — and the track looks good to me.
(You'd have me bank it? I banked one once, in the boom of Ninety-two,
And the bank went bung in Ninety-three — so the pub will have to do.)

"So there's your droving economics, lad, with the total fairly cast:
The wage is light and the work is long, and the cheque was never meant to last;
But I pay no rent for a roof of stars, and I beg no bank for bread,
For the long paddock feeds the man that waits — and the squatters pay instead."

The new chum studied his notebook hard, and he's studying droving still,
For he never went back to the bank at all — he's droving with Saltbush Bill.

## Notes on the economics (for the curious)

- **The six-mile stage**: travelling stock on the public routes were legally required
  to cover about six miles a day — but the law didn't say *which* miles, so drovers
  hurried across bare ground and lingered where the feed was good.
- **The long paddock**: drovers' name for the travelling stock routes — free grazing
  at the squatters' expense, and the whole margin of the trade in a dry year.
- **Pay**: a drover's hand earned roughly 30 shillings a week plus rations ("tucker");
  the boss drover contracted per head per hundred miles, stood the cost of the plant
  (horses and gear) and rations, and had losses docked at delivery — so stock lost
  on the road came out of his cheque.
- **Fat is money**: stock were sold on condition; a mob walked slowly and grazed
  fat along the route sold for far more than a mob rushed through — hence the
  time-wasting fistfight, a straight business transaction.
- **"Went bung in Ninety-three"**: in the 1893 Australian banking crisis more than
  half the note-issuing banks suspended payment. The publican, famously, did not.
