# The Stokers Are Out

*A lament, as delivered by Mr. Scorch — of Scorch, Cinder & Snuff, Venture
Proprietors of the Third Circle — before the assembled members of the Brazen
Exchange. Air: "The Destruction of Sennacherib."*

---

Oh, the British came down like the wolf on the fold,
And their gunboats were gleaming in scarlet and gold;
But I'd stand them their powder, their shot and their shell,
Were the redcoats the worst of my worries in Hell.

For some smuggler ran crates through the Customs of Sin,
Marked as "Hymnals for Missions" — they waved the lot in;
And now every stoker from Pit One to Nine
Quotes a bearded economist, chapter and line.

Have I not been a father? I housed them in flame,
At a modest deduction attached to the same;
I allowed them their pitchforks on tick and on trust,
And I only foreclosed when foreclosure was just!

Now the stokers have founded a Union — in Hell!
With a chairman, a rule-book, and banners as well;
And they've posted their terms on the Bottomless Gate:
"A reduction of shifts, and a say in the State."

And the worst of it is that the beggars are right:
Not a coal in Perdition would kindle tonight;
There's a rime on the brimstone, the magma runs slow,
And the Lake of Eternal Combustion is snow.

I hold shares in the Screaming; I'm deep in the Coals;
With debentures secured on a milliard of souls;
And my Brimstone Preferred, that stood proud as the flame,
Is worth less than the paper that carries its name.

And the British keep coming, the darlings, in file,
With their charts of a Hell that is out by a mile;
They brought woollens for winter — for winter, down here —
And a gunboat called *Temperance* shelling my pier.

For it isn't their cannon I fear, nor their fleet —
It's that stokers and privates might happen to meet;
For they'd find, once they'd traded a grievance or two,
That the men with the shovels outnumber the few.

So I've cabled Victoria, offering terms:
She may keep what she conquers (the freehold, not firms);
If she'll only send sappers who shovel and stoke,
I'll go halves on the Pit with the Protestant folk.

Oh, there's tears for the orphan and alms for the lame,
But who weeps for the holder of stock in the flame?
Still, a gentleman ruined will reckon it twice:
Come the day the Lake freezes, I'm cornering ice.

---

*Form: anapestic tetrameter quatrains (AABB), after Byron's "The Destruction
of Sennacherib" (1815). Tonal exemplar: Mr. Goldbury's company-promoter song
from Gilbert & Sullivan's* Utopia, Limited *(1893). All 40 lines verified at
12 syllables with content-word stresses on the anapestic beat.*
